Part A · Getting in

01Signing in

Who does this: everyone

Open the program. On a computer it is the Jyothi Foods icon on the desktop; on a phone it is the web address the office gives you. You will see two boxes.

  1. Type your email address in the first box. This is the one the office created for you — not your personal Gmail unless they used it.
  2. Type your password in the second box. Nobody else can see it; it shows as dots.
  3. Press Sign in.

You stay signed in on that computer. You only have to do this again if you sign out, or if you have not opened it for many days.

Never share your password. Everything you do is recorded under your name — every bill, every receipt, every change to a rate. If you lend your login to someone, their mistakes become yours. If you think somebody knows it, tell the owner and it will be changed the same day.

Forgot your password? Only the owner or admin can reset it, from Setup › Users. There is no self-service reset by design — it is one fewer way in for a stranger.

02What the screen looks like

Who does this: everyone

Three parts, and they never move:

  • The left strip is the menu. It is grouped — Overview, Masters, Transactions, Operations, Reports & Setup. Click a name to go there.
  • The top bar shows the shop name and your own name. Your name is also the button for signing out.
  • The middle is the screen you chose.

You will not see every menu item. The system shows only what your job needs. A production supervisor does not see Payments; a driver sees almost nothing on the computer because their work is on the phone. This is not a fault. If you genuinely need a screen you cannot see, ask the owner to tick it for you in Setup › Permissions.

Two words used everywhere in this handbook. Masters are the lists you set up once and reuse forever — products, customers, sections. Transactions are the things that happen daily — bills, receipts, purchases. Get the masters right and the transactions become quick.

03The first-day wizard

Who does this: owner, admin

The very first time the shop uses the system, go to Setup › Wizard. It walks you through the setup screens in the right order, one at a time, so nothing is missed. Order matters here: you cannot put a product into a section that does not exist yet.

The wizard's order is the correct order, and it is also the order of Part B of this handbook:

  1. Business details
  2. Units of measure
  3. Pack types
  4. Receipt modes and expense heads
  5. Users (including mestris)
  6. Sections
  7. Locations and routes
  8. Numbering
  9. Permissions

You do not have to finish it in one sitting. Leave it and come back; it remembers where you stopped. You can also skip the wizard entirely and use the tabs on Setup directly — the wizard is just the same tabs in a safe order.

Part B · Setting the shop up

04Business details

Who does this: owner, admin

Setup › Business. This is what prints at the top of every bill, so get it exactly right.

  • Shop name — as it should appear on a bill and on the rate card.
  • Address, phone, email — the ones a customer should use to reach you.
  • GSTIN — if you bill with GST. Leave blank if you do not.
  • Logo — click, choose the picture file, done. It appears on bills and on the rate card. A square or wide picture works; a very tall one gets squeezed.

Change these any time. Old bills already printed do not change — only new ones.

05Units of measure

Who does this: owner, admin

Unit of measure
కొలత
kolata
సరుకును దేనితో లెక్కపెడతామో అది — కిలో, గ్రాము, లీటరు, పీసు, పెట్టె.
How you count a thing: kilogram, gram, litre, piece, box.

Setup › Units. Add every unit you actually use. Typically: KG, G, L, ML, PCS, BOX.

Each unit has a short code (KG) and a name (Kilogram). The code is what appears in narrow columns on screen and on printed sheets, so keep it to two or three letters.

Why this matters. Stock is counted in the unit you pick here. If laddu is set to KG, every stock figure for laddu is in kilograms — a count sheet that says 42 means 42 kg. Change a product's unit after stock has moved and the old numbers stop meaning the same thing, so decide once.

06Pack types

Who does this: owner, admin

Pack type
ప్యాకింగ్ రకం
packing rakam
సరుకు ఏ రూపంలో అమ్ముతున్నామో అది — పాకెట్, డబ్బా, కవర్, లూజ్.
The shape the product is sold in: packet, tin, cover, loose.

Setup › Pack types. Add the ones the shop uses: Packet, Box, Tin, Cover, Loose, Jar.

The pack type is not the same as the unit. A unit is how much; a pack type is what it is in. The same 250 g of mixture can be a Packet or Loose, and the rate card prints "₹60 per packet" because of what you set here.

07Sections and mestris

Who does this: owner, admin

This is the word people ask about most, so it gets the longest explanation.

Section
విభాగం
vibhāgam
తయారీలో ఒక భాగం. ఒక్కో విభాగానికి ఒక మేస్త్రీ ఉంటాడు. ఉదాహరణకు: స్వీట్స్ విభాగం, హాట్ ఐటమ్స్ విభాగం, ప్యాకింగ్ విభాగం. ఆ విభాగంలో తయారయ్యే సరుకు అంతా ఆ మేస్త్రీ లెక్కలోకి వస్తుంది.
One part of the factory, run by one mestri. Everything made in that part is counted against that mestri.
Mestri
మేస్త్రీ
mestrī
ఒక విభాగాన్ని నడిపే పెద్ద పనివాడు. ఆ విభాగం పని, సరుకు, తేడాలు అన్నీ అతని బాధ్యత.
The head workman who runs a section and answers for its output.

What a section is, in plain words

Think of the factory floor divided into areas. The sweets area. The hot-items area. The packing area. Each area has a mestri in charge. A section is the system's name for one of those areas.

Once sections exist, three things become possible that were not possible before:

  • Every product belongs to a section, so you can ask how much did the sweets section produce this month.
  • Production batches are booked against a section, so wastage and shortfall have an owner.
  • The rate card and the sales reports group by section, which is how customers and the owner both think.

Adding a section

Mestris are users, so add the users first (chapter 8), then come back here. Otherwise there is nobody to pick.

  1. Go to Setup › Sections.
  2. Press Add section.
  3. Name — what people call it: Sweets, Hot, Packing, Namkeen.
  4. Code — two or three letters: SW, HOT, PKG.
  5. Mestri — pick from the list. Only users ticked as a mestri appear here.
  6. Sort order — a number that decides the order sections appear everywhere: on the rate card, in reports, in the item list. Give the biggest section 1.
  7. Press Save.

A mestri can change. If a section changes hands, edit the section and pick the new person. Old batches keep the old mestri's name, which is correct — they did that work.

08Users and who sees what

Who does this: owner only

Setup › Users. One entry per person who works with the system.

  1. Press Add user.
  2. Name — the person's real name, as you would say it out loud.
  3. Email and password — these are their login, and they are optional. Leave both blank for anyone who will never sign in: a driver whose work is the trip sheet, a mestri named on sections and batches, anyone who only needs to be picked from a list or counted in the attendance register. Do not invent an email to get past the form — an invented email is a login nobody controls. Give a login only to people who will actually open the program, and then fill in both boxes.
  4. Role — see the table below.
  5. Tick Is a mestri (runs a section) if they head a section.
  6. Press Save.

Mestri is a tick, not a role. It sits on top of whatever role you gave them. A mestri with the production role can enter batches; a mestri with no login at all still gets counted in section reports. The tick only decides one thing: whether their name appears in the mestri list on Setup › Sections.

The roles

RoleTeluguWhat they can do
OwnerయజమానిEverything, including users, licence and deleting records. Usually one person.
Adminమేనేజర్Everything day-to-day: bills, receipts, purchases, stock, setup. Cannot change the licence.
Accountantఅకౌంటెంట్Bills, receipts, payments, accounts, reports, attendance. Not setup.
Productionతయారీ ఇన్‌ఛార్జిBatches, recipes, stock. Does not see money screens.
Driverడ్రైవర్Their own trip on the phone: stops, van sales, collections. Nothing else.

Need something finer — an accountant who may view purchases but not change them? Setup › Permissions has a grid: one row per screen, tick boxes for view, edit and delete. Change a role there and it applies to everyone with that role at once.

When someone leaves. Do not delete them — their name is on hundreds of past bills and those must stay honest. Set them to inactive instead. They can no longer sign in, and the history stays intact.

09Locations, routes, numbering

Who does this: owner, admin

Locations

Location
స్థలం / గోడౌన్
sthalam / godown
సరుకు ఎక్కడ ఉందో ఆ చోటు — ఫ్యాక్టరీ, షాపు, గోడౌన్, వ్యాన్.
A place stock physically sits: factory, shop, godown, van.

Setup › Locations. Every stock figure is per location, so the system can answer "how much laddu is at the shop" separately from "how much is in the godown". Add at least one. Most shops have two or three.

Routes

Route
రూట్ / మార్గం
route / mārgam
వ్యాన్ ఒక రోజు తిరిగే దారి, అందులో వచ్చే ఊళ్లు, షాపులు.
The path a van covers in a day, with the towns and shops on it.

Setup › Routes. Give each route a name people already use — Vijayawada Road, Town, Guntur Line. Customers get attached to a route (chapter 15). After that, route-wise sales and route profit reports work on their own.

Numbering

Setup › Numbering. Decides how bill numbers look. Set a prefix (JF/25-26/) and a starting number, and every new invoice, receipt, purchase and challan numbers itself.

Set this before the first bill. Changing the prefix later leaves a visible break in the book — JF/001 to JF/040 then JFN/041. Auditors ask about breaks. Decide it once, at the start.

The form money arrived in: cash, UPI, cheque, bank transfer.

Setup › Receipt modes. Add Cash, UPI, Cheque, Bank transfer, and any name your own staff use for a mode. Later, Reports › Collection by mode tells you how much came in each way — which is only as accurate as this list.

Part C · Products

11Adding a product

Who does this: owner, admin

Item / Product
సరుకు
saruku
మీరు తయారు చేసి అమ్మే వస్తువు, లేదా తయారీకి కొనే ముడి సరుకు.
A thing you make and sell, or a raw material you buy to make it.

Go to Items and press New item. Fill the form top to bottom.

  1. Item name. What everyone calls it. "Boondi Laddu 250g", not "BL250". Say it out loud — if a new worker would understand it, it is right.
  2. Item code. A short code for narrow screens and count sheets. Leave it blank and the system makes one.
  3. Type. Finished for what you sell, Raw material for what you buy to make it (besan, oil, sugar), Packing for covers and boxes. This decides which screens the item appears on — a raw material must not turn up on a customer's bill.
  4. Section (mestri). Which part of the factory makes it. Chapter 7 explains sections.
  5. Stock kept in. The unit stock is counted in — KG for loose, PCS for pieces. Chapter 5.
  6. Pack type. Packet, box, tin, loose. Chapter 6.
  7. Pieces per box and units per box. How many go in a carton. Needed for box rates and for stock to add up when someone sells by the box.
  8. Unit rate. Your normal selling price for one unit.
  9. Box rate. The price when sold by the full box. Leave blank if you never sell that way.
  10. Purchase rate. What it costs you. This is what makes the profit reports mean anything. Nobody outside owner and accountant sees it.
  11. MRP per piece. The printed maximum price, if the pack carries one.
  12. Net weight (g). For loose items sold by weight.
  13. Reorder level. When stock drops below this number, the item shows on Stock › Low stock. Set it to about a week's selling.
  14. Shelf life (days). How many days it stays good. Drives expiry warnings on batches.
  15. Press Save.

You do not need every field. Name, type, unit and rate are enough to start billing. The rest can be filled later by opening the item again. But units per box and purchase rate are the two that quietly break reports when left empty — fill them when you have a quiet hour.

Stopping a product. Do not delete an item that has ever been billed or stocked — the old bills need it. Open it and untick Active. It vanishes from new bills and from the rate card, and every past record still reads correctly.

12Adding a product photo

Who does this: owner, admin

A photo makes the rate card worth sending. Adding one takes about fifteen seconds.

  1. Go to Items and click the product's name to open it. (For a brand-new product you can add the photo straight away, before saving.)
  2. Find the Photo box at the top of the form.
  3. Press Choose photo. Your computer's file window opens.
  4. Pick the picture and press Open. It uploads immediately and you see it appear in the box.
  5. Press Save at the bottom of the form. The photo is only attached to the product once you save.

The one mistake people make. The photo appearing in the box does not mean it is saved. You must press Save on the product form afterwards. If you upload a photo and then click away to another screen, the photo is lost and you will have to do it again.

Changing or removing a photo

Open the item. Press Replace and pick a different picture, or press Remove the photo to clear it. Either way, press Save.

Taking a good product photo

  • Daylight, near a window or in shade outdoors. No flash — it whitens sweets.
  • Plain background. A steel tray or a white sheet is plenty.
  • Fill the frame with the product. Nobody needs to see the table.
  • Photograph one product at a time, so the picture matches the name.
  • A normal phone photo is the right size. Anything over 5 MB is refused — if that happens, send the photo to yourself on WhatsApp and use the copy WhatsApp produces, which is smaller.
  • JPG and PNG are accepted. HEIC from an iPhone may not be — the WhatsApp trick fixes that too.

Do it in one sitting. Lay out twenty products on a tray by the window, photograph them one after another, then add them all in one go. An afternoon gets the whole catalogue done, and the rate card is transformed.

13The rate card

Who does this: owner, admin, accountant

Rate card / catalogue
రేటు పట్టిక
rēṭu paṭṭika
సరుకుల ఫోటో, ప్యాకింగ్, రేటుతో ఉన్న పట్టిక — కస్టమర్‌కు చూపించడానికి, పంపడానికి.
A sheet of products with photo, pack and price, to show or send a customer.

Go to Rate card in the Masters group. It builds itself from your products — there is nothing to type.

  1. Section — choose one section, or leave it on "Every section" for the full list.
  2. Only products with a photo — leave this ticked. A card of blank squares looks worse than a shorter card.
  3. Show rates — untick to print a picture-only sheet, for a customer who negotiates rates separately.
  4. Check the count on the right: "24 products". If it says 0, no product in that section has a photo yet.
  5. Press Print.

Sending it on WhatsApp

In the print window that opens, change the printer to Save as PDF (on Windows: "Microsoft Print to PDF"), press Save, and put the file somewhere you can find it. Then send that PDF from WhatsApp Web or from your phone like any other file.

Rates are the product's own rates. Not any one customer's special price. That is deliberate — a card priced for one shop that reaches another causes an argument you cannot win. For customer-specific pricing use price lists (chapter 14) and quote from a bill instead.

The card carries your shop logo, name, the section, and today's date at the top, and a line at the bottom saying rates may change. That line matters: it is what lets you raise a rate next month without a customer waving an old sheet at you.

14Price lists

Who does this: owner, admin  ·  Growth plan and above

Price list
ప్రత్యేక రేట్ల పట్టిక
pratyēka rēṭla paṭṭika
కొంతమంది కస్టమర్లకు మాత్రమే వర్తించే ప్రత్యేక రేట్లు.
Special rates that apply only to certain customers.

Pricing. Make a list — "Wholesale", "Town retail", "Guntur dealers" — and set the rate for the products that differ from your normal rate. You do not have to list every product; anything you leave out uses the item's own rate.

Then attach the list to a customer (chapter 15). From then on, every bill for that customer picks their rate automatically. No remembering, no arguments, no rate typed wrong at seven in the evening.

Part D · Selling

15Customers

Who does this: owner, admin, accountant

Customers, then New customer.

  1. Name — the shop's name, and the owner's name in brackets if that is how your staff refer to them: "Sri Venkateswara Stores (Ramu)".
  2. Phone — the WhatsApp number. Reminders and order confirmations go here, so get it right.
  3. Town and Route — which van run covers them. This is what makes route reports and the driver's stop list work.
  4. Price list — if they get special rates.
  5. Credit limit — the most you are willing to let them owe. When they cross it, billing warns you.
  6. Opening balance — what they already owed on the day you started using the system. Enter it once, never again.
  7. Save.
Outstanding
బాకీ
bākī
కస్టమర్ ఇంకా ఇవ్వాల్సిన డబ్బు. బిల్లు వేస్తే పెరుగుతుంది, వసూలు చేస్తే తగ్గుతుంది.
What a customer still owes. A bill increases it; a receipt reduces it.

Open any customer to see their running balance and every bill and receipt against them. That screen is the answer to "how much does he owe?" — no notebook needed.

16Quotations

Who does this: owner, admin, accountant  ·  Growth plan and above

Quotation
ధర పత్రం
dhara patram
"ఇంత రేటుకు ఇస్తాం" అని ముందుగా ఇచ్చే కాగితం. ఇది బిల్లు కాదు — డబ్బు బాకీ అవదు.

17Orders

Who does this: owner, admin, accountant  ·  Growth plan and above

Order
ఆర్డర్
order
కస్టమర్ అడిగిన సరుకు జాబితా. ఇంకా పంపలేదు, బిల్లు వేయలేదు.
What a customer has asked for. Not yet sent, not yet billed.

Orders. Type an order as it comes in on the phone, or open one that arrived by WhatsApp (chapter 31). Production looks at pending orders to know what to make tomorrow, and the loading list is built from them.

When goods go out, open the order and press Convert to invoice or Convert to challan. Orders that are partly delivered stay open for the balance, so nothing is quietly forgotten.

The same screen has a purchase orders side, at Orders › Purchase, for what you order from your own suppliers.

18Challans

Who does this: owner, admin, accountant  ·  Growth plan and above

Challan
డెలివరీ చలానా
delivery chalānā
సరుకుతో పాటు పంపే కాగితం. ఏమేమి పంపామో చెబుతుంది, కానీ రేట్లు, డబ్బు ఉండవు.
The paper that travels with the goods. Says what was sent; no prices, no money.

Use a challan when goods must move before the bill is settled — sale-or-return, goods sent for approval, a load going out while the rate is still being agreed. Stock leaves your books; the customer's account is untouched.

Later, open the challan and press Convert to invoice for whatever they kept.

19Sales invoices

Who does this: owner, admin, accountant

Invoice / Bill
బిల్లు
billu
అమ్మకం పక్కా అయ్యాక ఇచ్చే కాగితం. కస్టమర్ బాకీ పెరుగుతుంది, నిల్వ తగ్గుతుంది.
The document for a confirmed sale. It raises what the customer owes and lowers stock.

This is the screen most used, so it is built to be fast. Sales Invoices › New invoice.

  1. Customer. Start typing the name; the list narrows. Their rates load automatically, and their current outstanding shows at the top so you know what you are walking into.
  2. Date. Today unless you change it.
  3. Add a line. Start typing a product name and pick it. Type the quantity. The rate fills itself in — from the customer's price list if they have one, otherwise the item's own rate.
  4. Change the rate only for a genuine one-off. A rate you change on every bill belongs in a price list instead.
  5. More lines. Repeat. There is no limit.
  6. Discount — on a single line, or on the whole bill at the bottom.
  7. Check the total. It adds up as you go.
  8. Press Save. The bill takes its number, stock comes down, and the customer's outstanding goes up.

Correcting a bill. Open it and press Edit invoice. See below — it is safe, and the bill keeps its number.

Changing a bill you have already saved

Open the invoice from the list and press Edit invoice at the top. Confirm the message, change what is wrong, and press Save then confirm it again.

What the system does behind that button, so nobody has to wonder:

  • The goods go back into stock, so the shelf figure is right while you are editing.
  • The accounts entry is reversed, so the customer does not owe for the bill twice.
  • The bill goes back to being a draft so its lines can be changed.
  • Confirming again posts the corrected stock and the corrected amount, once.
  • The invoice number does not change. That is the whole point — the customer already has that number on their copy.

Two things stop an edit, on purpose. If a receipt has been entered against the bill, or a return raised on it, the screen refuses and says which. Undo that first. Money and goods that have already moved must be unwound deliberately by a person who can see what they are unwinding — not silently by a button.

Every version is kept. Setup › Audit trail records who edited the bill, when, and what changed. Correcting an honest mistake is normal; the record of it is what keeps the book defensible.

20Printing a bill

Who does this: everyone who bills

  1. Open the invoice from the list, or stay on it after saving.
  2. Press Print. A clean bill page opens — no menu, no buttons, just the bill.
  3. Your computer's print window appears. Check the printer, press Print.

To send instead of print, choose Save as PDF in the same window and share the file on WhatsApp.

Changing how the bill looks

Setup › Print designer controls the printed layout: which columns appear, the terms line at the bottom, the signature block, paper size. Set it once with the owner, print one test bill, and leave it alone.

22Receipts — collecting money

Receipt
వసూలు రసీదు

Receipts › New receipt.

  1. Customer. Their outstanding appears immediately.
  2. Amount received.
  3. Mode — cash, UPI, cheque, bank transfer. From the list you set in chapter 10.
  4. Reference — cheque number, UPI transaction id. Skip for cash.
  5. Against which bills. The open bills are listed. Tick the ones this money settles, or let it settle the oldest first, which is the default and usually right.

Cheques

A cheque received is not money until it clears. Enter it with mode Cheque; it sits in Accounts › Cheques as pending. When the bank clears it, mark it cleared there. If it bounces, mark it bounced — the customer's outstanding goes straight back up, which is exactly what should happen.

26Stock

Who does this: owner, admin, production

Stock
నిల్వ
nilva
ఇప్పుడు మీ దగ్గర ఎంత సరుకు ఉందో అది.
How much of each thing you have right now.

Nobody types stock figures. Stock moves on its own: a purchase or a production batch raises it, a bill or a challan lowers it. Stock just shows the result.

TabWhat it is for
Closing stockWhat you have now, per item and per location. The screen you open most.
MovementsEvery in and out for one item. Use it when a figure looks wrong — it shows exactly which document moved it.
Low stockItems below their reorder level. Check it before ordering raw material.
TransfersMoving stock factory to shop, or shop to van. Print the transfer slip and send it with the goods.
Stock countPhysical counting. See below.
BarcodesPrint labels for packs and shelves.

Doing a physical count

  1. Stock › Stock count, press New count, choose the location.
  2. Print the count sheet. It lists every item with a blank column.
  3. Walk the godown and write the real quantities by hand.
  4. Come back, open the count and type what you wrote.
  5. The screen shows book quantity against counted quantity, and the difference.
  6. Look at every difference before you press Post. A difference is usually a missed entry, not missing goods.
  7. Post makes the system agree with the shelf.

Do not post a count you have not looked at. Posting overwrites the book figure. If the real reason for a gap was an unentered purchase, posting hides that mistake instead of fixing it — and the supplier's outstanding stays wrong.

27Production

Who does this: production, admin, owner

Batch
బ్యాచ్ / తయారీ

ఒకసారి చేసిన తయారీ — ఎంత ముడి సరుకు వాడారు, ఎంత సరుకు వచ్చింది.

Recipe
తయారీ విధానం
tayārī vidhānam

The standing list of what and how much goes into making one product.
Variance
తేడా
tēḍā
రావాల్సిన దానికి, నిజంగా వచ్చిన దానికి మధ్య తేడా.
The gap between what should have come out and what did.

Set the recipes once

Production › Recipes. For each finished product, list the raw materials and how much of each makes one standard batch. Do this with the mestri standing next to you — they know the real quantities, not the book ones.

Record a batch each time you make something

  1. Production › Batches, press New batch.
  2. Pick the product and the section. The mestri fills in from the section.
  3. Enter the planned quantity. The recipe fills the raw material list automatically.
  4. Print the batch sheet and give it to the mestri. It lists what to draw from the godown.
  5. After the run, enter the actual output and any changes to what was actually used.
  6. Post. Raw material comes out of stock, finished goods go in.

Reading variance

Production › Variance compares recipe against reality, by section and by mestri. A small gap every time is normal — frying loses weight. A gap that suddenly doubles is a question worth asking, and this screen is what lets you ask it with a number instead of a suspicion.

Who does this: admin, owner

ఒక వ్యాన్ ఒక రోజు చేసే పని — ఎంత సరుకు తీసుకెళ్లింది, ఎక్కడెక్కడ ఆగింది, ఎంత అమ్మింది, ఎంత వసూలు చేసింది, ఎంత తిరిగి వచ్చింది.

Add each vehicle once at Vehicles — number, driver, route.

Starting the day

  1. Vehicles › Trips, press New trip.
  2. Pick vehicle, driver, route and date.

29The driver's phone

Who does this: driver  ·  Growth plan and above

The driver opens the same web address on their phone and signs in with their own login. They get a simple screen with big buttons, not the office layout.

30Attendance and Punchly

Who does this: admin, accountant, owner

Attendance
హాజరు
hājaru
ఎవరు ఏ రోజు వచ్చారు, ఎన్ని గంటలు పనిచేశారు అనే లెక్క.
Who came which day, and how many hours they worked.

How it works

Workers mark themselves present in the Punchly app on their own phone — they open it, press punch in when they arrive and punch out when they leave. Punchly records the time and the place.

The ERP fetches those punches by itself several times a day. Nobody types attendance, and workers never touch the ERP.

What the office does

  1. Open Attendance. The day's register is already filled: in-time, out-time, hours worked.
  2. Look at the rows marked needs review. These are the ones the system is not sure about — a missing punch-out, a very short day, a punch from far away from the factory.
  3. Fix them. Press Enter a day to type an attendance by hand for someone whose phone died, or who genuinely was not on the app.
  4. Open the Wages tab at month end to see days and hours per person for paying.

Sync now pulls fresh punches immediately, for when someone punched in two minutes ago and you want to see it.

New worker

  1. Add them in Punchly and let them install the app.
  2. Add them at Setup › Users in the ERP.
  3. Go to Setup › Attendance and link the Punchly name to the ERP name. Once linked, it stays linked.

Unlinked people show no attendance. If somebody's row is empty all month, the usual reason is that step 3 was never done. Check the link list first, before anything else.

The day runs on Indian time. A punch at 11:45 pm belongs to that day, not the next. You do not have to think about it — but it is why a night-shift register looks right rather than split in half.

33Backup and audit trail

Who does this: owner

Backup

The system backs itself up every night with no help from anyone. Setup › Backup shows when the last one ran.

Before anything big — a stock count, a rate revision across the board, importing old data — press Backup now and take one anyway. It costs a minute.

Download saves a copy to your own computer. Do that once a month and keep it somewhere off the office machine.

Photos are not inside the backup. The backup holds all your records and the links to your product photos, but the picture files themselves live separately. They are not lost when you restore — but do not let anyone empty the photo storage thinking a backup covers it.

38The daily routine

If you only remember one page of this handbook, make it this one.

Every morning

  • Open the Dashboard. Yesterday's sales, today's collections due, low stock — all on one screen.
  • Check Attendance for rows marked needs review.
  • Start the day's trips and print the loading sheets.

Through the day

  • Bills as the goods go out. Not in the evening from a notebook.
  • Receipts the moment the money is taken.
  • Purchase bills the day they arrive.

Every evening

  • Close the trips. Returns in, collections checked against the cash.
  • Post the day's production batches.
  • Open Accounts › Day book and check it against the cash box.

Every week

  • Reports › Outstanding ageing — the collection list.
  • Stock › Low stock — before the purchase run.

Every month

  • A physical stock count.
  • Attendance › Wages for the pay run.
  • Reports › Route profit and the sales reports.
  • Download one backup to your own computer.

The one rule underneath all of it. Enter things when they happen, not later. A system fed the same day answers every question in seconds. A system fed on Saturday from memory is just a slower notebook.
